# Hackathon

VIBloom was born for the Meta AI Hackathon 2026 (Indie track, theme: Push the
Frontier).

The hackathon ran for 10 days. I couldn't start until November 2 because of
personal stuff, so for me it was 3 days of very intense work, from the first
commit to the submission. Not ideal, but it was what I had, and I put
everything I had into it.

## It wasn't a random idea

VIBloom wasn't a vague idea or a bit of AI slop thrown together to have
something to submit. Even on the days I couldn't touch the code, I was thinking
about it, a lot. The question I kept asking myself was: what would actually
push the frontier?

My answer, from everything I've lived and worked on, is assumptions. The
biases and assumptions we carry without noticing, the ones humans have by
nature and the ones today's AI has too. That is where most of our mistakes come
from. Nobody checks what everybody takes for granted.

The tree was for my country: the guanacaste is Costa Rica's national tree. And
honestly, it was also a way to stand out. The tree is going away now. The roots
are staying. The roots were always the point: every conclusion sits on
foundations, and those foundations are what deserve to be questioned.

I really believe that, done right, VIBloom can push the frontier. Not by being
perfect, but by going after the things everyone assumes, and letting AI do the
heavy lifting of checking them. That is my vision, that is my ambition, and
that is what I'm going to keep working on.

## The result

VIBloom wasn't a finalist. The judges made their call and I respect it. I just
wish there had been some feedback, any feedback. After burning my head on this
for days, a couple of lines saying what didn't land would have meant more to me
than the result itself. That never came.

I tried to make the vision clear in the demo and in the docs. Looking at the
winners, VIBloom just wasn't what the judges, or whatever algorithm screened
it, were looking for.

## What changes now

I focused VIBloom on chemistry on purpose, to have one niche where I could
actually prove something in the time I had. The idea is much bigger than
chemistry: take a research question, break it into what it rests on, test each
piece, and keep every conclusion tied to its evidence.

VIBloom was shaped around the hackathon's rules, and one of them was to use
only Meta's models. That rule is gone now. Muse got me through the hackathon,
but it isn't the strongest model out there, and a research tool deserves the
best tools available, not the ones a contest requires. So from here on I'm
changing the structure and choosing every tool on its merits.
